Kickoff Pulse · Product vision & roadmap

The analysis layer youth football gets to keep.

From a tool that records a match to the system a club owns outright — trustworthy first, shareable second, institutional third.

18,500 lines 213 tests v1.18.0 shipped 1 coach, 1 maintainer
01The position

Local-first is the moat, not a preference

Nothing leaves the machine. That has been treated as a technical choice. It is the strongest strategic position the product has, and it should be stated as one.

This is footage and performance data about children. Every cloud rival asks a club to upload video of minors to a vendor's servers, under that vendor's retention policy, in another jurisdiction, with access controlled by someone the club will never meet. That is a problem for them and an advantage here — one that grows as youth-sports data protection tightens, not one that erodes.

The club owns its data, literally

It sits on their machines. No export fight, no vendor lock-in, no “we're sunsetting this product” e-mail.

It works where football is played

Rural pitches have no signal. Capture already never depends on the network — that was a correctness decision, and it is also the reason it works when a cloud product would not.

Honesty is a feature

Every camera run is graded measured, indicative or unusable, with the reason stated. Youth analytics is full of confident-looking numbers from bad data. An app that says when not to trust it earns something no marketing claim can.

Each unserved audience is also a distribution channel. A parent forwards a clip of their own child; a Director of Coaching who can see across age groups asks every coach to use it. Nothing spreads a coaching tool faster than a family seeing something about their own kid.

03Horizons

Be trustworthy, then shareable, then institutional

The order is deliberate. A club platform built on numbers nobody trusts is worse than no club platform.

Horizon 1Next few months

Make the promise true

Reliability and honesty before scale. A club that adopts a tool which fails on match day never adopts it twice.

  • Clear the vision ceiling Blocked
    Ball detection sits at 4–12%, so possession and passing grade indicative and everything spatial stays out of reach. The highest-leverage task in the roadmap.
  • Prove match day Blocked
    A full live soak: token expiry, reconnects, thermals, fps decay across 90 minutes. Still the biggest match-day unknown.
  • Close the coaching loop Next
    Opponent scouting from matches already archived; training links that turn “we conceded three from our left” into a session; in-match assistance the console can already derive.
Horizon 2~6 months

From coach tool to team platform

  • Give the player their own surface. Today they receive a pack if a coach makes one. They should have their own read-only view — season, clips, trend, playing time.
  • Take safeguarding seriously. A prerequisite, not a feature. See below.
  • Team workflows. Multiple staff, roles, shared notes, a handover when someone else runs the touchline.
  • Make it installable. The app is unsigned. macOS warns any coach who isn't you, which ends most adoptions at the first click.
The safeguarding gap. The app holds video and performance data about minors and has no consent record, no retention policy, and no way to delete a player — only a match. At single-coach scale that is a gap. At club scale it is a liability, and it undercuts the exact privacy position that makes this distinctive. Local-first makes it easier to solve than the cloud alternative: there is one place to delete from. That is the point.
Horizon 3~12 months

The club platform

  • Player pathways. “How did this player develop from U12 to U16?” needs identity that persists across teams and seasons — which is why jersey identity matters far beyond one match.
  • Club standards. Comparable data across age groups. The season layer already computes most pieces per team; the work is aggregation and permissioning, not new analysis.
  • Evidence for a board. Participation, development and coaching quality — mostly assembly over data already held, and what makes a club pay attention.
  • Coach development, handled carefully. The same data that shows player progress implies coach effectiveness. Done well it supports coaches; done badly it is surveillance and destroys adoption. Opt-in, coach-first, never a league table of adults.
04Keystones

Two things gate everything, and neither is code

Blocked

A 1080p Veo export

Unlocks the retrain → ball detection → possession, passing, shape, pathways. Without it, roughly a third of this roadmap ships permanently graded indicative.

Blocked

A second real user

Every decision so far has been validated against one coach. The first outside coach will find assumptions invisible from inside — and Horizon 2 is largely guesswork until then.

05Hard truths

What could stop this

A solo maintainer is the biggest risk to club adoption

A club running its season on this depends on one person's evenings. Before selling anyone on Horizon 3, decide what happens if you stop: open-source with a community, a second maintainer, or explicitly a personal tool others may use at their own risk. Clubs are right to ask, and the answer changes what you should promise.

The vision ceiling is a data problem, not a code problem

It needs annotation work. No amount of engineering routes around it.

Scope discipline will decide this

The temptation at every horizon is to add a metric. The differentiator is not more numbers — it is trustworthy numbers, honestly labelled, that reach the right person.

Sustainability is unanswered

Free and local forever? Paid for clubs? Support contracts? It needs no answer today, but Horizon 3 assumes clubs depend on it, and dependency without a sustainability model is a promise that breaks.

06Not building

Deliberately out of scope

  • Live streaming or broadcast. Veo does it; competing there is a rout.
  • A cloud rewrite. It would trade away the entire defensible position.
  • Predictive scouting ratings. At this data volume they would be numerology, and on children they would be indefensible.
  • A native mobile app, until the sideline view proves phones matter. Read-only web is most of the value at a fraction of the cost.
  • More spatial metrics before the retrain. They would ship indicative and teach coaches to distrust everything else.

08Open

Questions only you can answer

Is this a product or a personal tool? Everything in Horizons 2–3 assumes other people depend on it. That is a commitment, not a feature.
Who owns a player's data in a club install? The club, the coach, or the family. This decides the whole safeguarding design and cannot be retrofitted.
What happens at the age boundary? A player turning 18, or leaving the club, needs a defined outcome for their data.
Is coach-effectiveness data ever surfaced upward? Say no and Horizon 3 is simpler and safer. Say yes and it needs its own careful design.
