# Kickoff Pulse — product vision and roadmap

**Where it stands:** ~18,500 lines, 213 tests, a working capture→report→library→
season loop, club mode with offline sync, and as of v1.18.0 clips, player
development and share packs.

**Where it goes:** from a tool that *records a match* to the analysis layer a
youth club actually gets to keep.

---

## 1. The position worth owning

Kickoff Pulse is local-first: no cloud, no API keys, nothing leaves the machine.
That has been treated as a technical preference. **It is the product's strongest
strategic position, and it should be stated as one.**

This is footage and performance data about **children**. Every cloud-based rival
asks a club to upload video of minors to a vendor's servers, under that vendor's
retention policy, in another jurisdiction, with access controlled by someone the
club will never meet. That is a problem for them and an advantage here — one that
grows as youth-sports data protection tightens, not one that erodes.

Three things follow, and they should drive everything below:

1. **The club owns its data, literally.** It sits on their machines. No export
   fight, no vendor lock-in, no "we're sunsetting this product" e-mail.
2. **It works where football is played.** Rural pitches have no signal. Capture
   already never depends on the network — that was a correctness decision, and
   it is also the reason it works when a cloud product would not.
3. **Honesty is a feature.** `quality.py` grades every camera run *measured*,
   *indicative* or *unusable* and says why. Youth sports analytics is full of
   confident-looking numbers derived from bad data. An app that tells a coach
   when not to trust it earns something no marketing claim can.

The one-line positioning: **the analysis layer youth football gets to keep.**

## 2. Four audiences

The product has only ever served one of these properly.

| Audience | What they want | Served today |
|---|---|---|
| **The coach** (you) | Know what happened; know what to work on | Well — this is the whole app |
| **The player** | See myself improving; watch my moments | Barely — a share pack, generated by someone else |
| **The team** | Consistency across staff; shared preparation | Thinly — club mode exists, team workflows do not |
| **The club** | Standards, player pathways, evidence for a board | Not at all |

Each unserved audience is also a distribution channel. A parent forwards a clip
of their own child; a Director of Coaching who can see across age groups asks
every coach to use it. Nothing spreads a coaching tool faster than a player's
family seeing something about their own kid.

## 3. Horizon 1 — Make the promise true (next few months)

Everything here is about the product being *reliable and honest* before it is
*bigger*. A club adopting a tool that fails on match day never adopts it twice.

**3.1 Clear the vision ceiling.** Ball detection sits at 4–12% on current
footage, so possession and passing grade *indicative* and every spatial feature
stays out of reach. The retrain (`PHASE6_VISION_PLAN.md`) is the single highest-
leverage task in the entire roadmap and is **blocked on one 1080p Veo export**.
Nothing else unlocks as much.

**3.2 Prove match day.** A full live soak on a real Veo stream — token expiry,
reconnects, thermal behaviour, fps decay over 90 minutes. Still unverified, and
still the biggest match-day unknown.

**3.3 Close the coaching loop.** The app tells you what happened; it does not
help you act.
- **Opponent scouting** — you play the same sides repeatedly; every previous
  meeting is already archived and unqueried.
- **Training links** — turn "we conceded three from our left" into a session
  focus. Ollama and the analyst are already there.
- **In-match assistance** — the console records but never advises. "Away have had
  three shots in eight minutes" is derivable live today.

**3.4 Sharpen what just shipped.** Clip windows are a guess until you have
watched a few. The share pack wants a real recipient flow.

## 4. Horizon 2 — From coach tool to team platform (~6 months)

**4.1 Give the player their own surface.** Today a player receives a pack *if a
coach makes one*. They should have their own read-only view: their season, their
clips, their trend, their playing time. This is the change that turns a coach's
tool into something a squad uses — and the sideline view already proves the
pattern (read-only, phone-shaped, access-controlled).

**4.2 Take safeguarding seriously — this is a prerequisite, not a feature.**

The app holds video and performance data about minors and has **no consent
record, no retention policy, and no way to delete a player**. You can delete a
match; you cannot honour "please remove my child." At single-coach scale that is
a gap. At club scale, with data shared between adults, it is a liability — and it
undercuts the exact privacy position that makes this product distinctive.

Needed: per-player consent state, a retention policy with automatic expiry,
genuine per-player deletion across matches, media and clips, and an access log
for who viewed what. Local-first makes this *easier* than the cloud alternative —
there is one place to delete from — which is the point.

**4.3 Team workflows.** Multiple staff on one team: shared notes, roles
(head coach, assistant, analyst), a handover when someone else runs the touchline.

**4.4 Make it installable.** The app is **unsigned and unnotarised**. macOS will
warn any coach who is not you, which ends most adoptions at the first click.
Signing, notarisation, and an update path are the difference between software you
run and software you can give away.

## 5. Horizon 3 — The club platform (~12 months)

**5.1 Player pathways.** A club's real question is "how did this player develop
from U12 to U16?" That needs player identity to persist across teams and seasons
— which is why jersey-number identity (Phase D) matters far beyond one match.

**5.2 Club standards and dashboards.** A Director of Coaching wants comparable
data across age groups: are we developing players, is playing time equitable, are
results improving. The season layer already computes most pieces per team; the
missing work is aggregation and permissioning, not new analysis.

**5.3 Evidence for a board.** Youth clubs answer to committees and funders.
Season reports covering participation, development and coaching quality are
mostly assembly over data already held — and they are what makes a club *pay*
attention.

**5.4 Coach development.** The most sensitive idea here, and worth stating
carefully: the same data that shows player progress also implies coach
effectiveness. Handled well it supports coaches; handled badly it becomes
surveillance and destroys adoption. Any such feature must be opt-in, visible to
the coach first, and never a league table of adults.

## 6. The two keystones

Almost everything above waits on one of these, and neither is code:

1. **A 1080p Veo export.** Unlocks the retrain → ball detection → possession,
   passing, shape, pathways. Without it, roughly a third of this roadmap ships
   permanently graded *indicative*.
2. **A second real user.** Every design decision so far has been validated
   against one coach. The first outside coach will find assumptions that are
   invisible from inside — and Horizon 2 is largely guesswork until then.

## 7. Hard truths

**Solo maintainer is the biggest risk to club adoption.** A club that runs its
season on this is depending on one person's evenings. Before selling anyone on
Horizon 3, decide what happens if you stop: is it open-source with a community,
is there a second maintainer, or is it explicitly a personal tool that others may
use at their own risk? Clubs are right to ask, and the answer changes what you
should promise.

**The vision ceiling is not a bug to be fixed by better code.** It is a data
problem needing annotation work. No amount of engineering routes around it.

**Scope discipline will decide this.** The temptation at every horizon is to add
a metric. The differentiator is not more numbers — it is trustworthy numbers,
honestly labelled, that reach the right person.

**Sustainability is unanswered.** Free and local forever? Paid for clubs? Support
contracts? This does not need answering now, but Horizon 3 assumes clubs depend
on it, and dependency without a sustainability model is a promise that breaks.

## 8. What not to build

- **Live streaming or broadcast.** Veo does it; competing there is a rout.
- **A cloud rewrite.** It would trade away the entire defensible position.
- **Predictive/AI scouting ratings.** On this data volume they would be
  numerology, and on children they would be indefensible.
- **A native mobile app**, until the sideline view proves phones matter. A
  read-only web view is most of the value at a fraction of the cost.
- **More spatial metrics before the retrain.** They would ship *indicative* and
  teach coaches to distrust everything else.

## 9. Sequencing

```
NOW      Retrain (BLOCKED: 1080p export)  ─┐
         Live soak (BLOCKED: stream URL)   ├─▶ trustworthy vision
         Opponent scouting                 │
         Training links                    ┘

H2       Player surface ──┐
         Safeguarding ────┼─▶ safe to put in front of families
         Team workflows   │
         Signed app ──────┘   (and safe to give away)

H3       Pathways ────────┐
         Club dashboards  ├─▶ a club platform
         Board reporting  ┘
```

The order is deliberate: **be trustworthy, then be shareable, then be
institutional.** A club platform built on numbers nobody trusts is worse than no
club platform.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Is this a product or a personal tool?** Everything in Horizons 2–3 assumes
   other people depend on it. That is a commitment, not a feature.
2. **Who owns a player's data in a club install** — the club, the coach, or the
   family? This decides the whole safeguarding design and cannot be retrofitted.
3. **What happens at the age boundary?** A player turning 18, or leaving the
   club, needs a defined outcome for their data.
4. **Is coach-effectiveness data ever surfaced upward?** Say no, and Horizon 3
   is simpler and safer. Say yes, and it needs its own careful design.
